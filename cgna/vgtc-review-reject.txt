  1AC Full Review

    The submission presents a modular, node-graph framework for avatar animation
                across XR devices. The manuscript is clearly structured, but the
                scientific contribution remains unclear. As written, the work
                primarily reads as an integration layer around proprietary SDKs
                (demonstrated only on Meta and XREAL), with limited evidence that the
                approach generalizes to other trackers or significantly improves
                cross-device application development.

    Technical transparency is also lacking. Critical nodes (retargeting, fusion/IK)
                are described only at a high level, making it difficult to distinguish
                original work from wrapped vendor code. The paper does not provide
                concrete interface schemas or data contracts, which complicates the
                assessment of contribution, reproducibility, and maintainability.
                Including concise code snippets or pseudocode and offering an
                anonymized artifact during review would enable a clearer evaluation of
                the framework.

    The introduction makes broad claims about what makes avatars effective that do not
                generally hold true. For example, the statement “To be effective,
                avatars must replicate a user’s body accurately and responsively
                across diverse contexts” overlooks evidence of effective embodiment
                without strict visuomotor synchrony (e.g., visuotactile synchrony).
                Several assertions also lack precise citations (e.g., the opening
                claim about “supporting natural interaction and enhancing presence”).
                The introduction would benefit from defining key terms and scope,
                including what the authors consider an “avatar” (full-body humanoid
                only, hands-only, or also non-humanoid representations).

    The evaluation hinges on a latency metric labeled “input-to-photon/interaction
                latency” that appears to exclude sensor sampling or
                runtime/compositor. Hence, it effectively reports app-level pipeline
                time rather than motion-to-photon. Without a component breakdown and a
                side-by-side baseline against vendor pipelines, the reported numbers
                are hard to interpret and do not reveal the overheads or benefits of
                the proposed architecture. While the analysis indicates when
                offloading is beneficial within this framework, the results are not
                transferable outside of it. Additionally, there is no evidence of
                usability or maintainability (e.g., switching control modes or device
                families), nor is there a minimal example illustrating how a developer
                would compose nodes and run a simple scene.

    The submitted video further weakens the technical case: the avatar’s feet are
                tilted relative to the ground plane, and there is noticeable whole-
                body pose jitter, suggesting instability or inaccuracy in the
                retargeting/IK pipeline. Overall, the system is practically
                interesting, but in its current state, the manuscript does not
                establish research novelty and exhibits visible quality issues. I
                recommend rejection for the research track.

    I would recommend that the authors submit it as a demo. Additionally, when adding
                comparisons to baselines and usability evaluation, the work can be a
                good contribution to the community in the future.

committee member review (reviewer 1)

  Summary of Contributions

    This paper presents a framework that enables to integrate into a unified approach
                tracking inputs, as well as proprietary avatar animation modules
                (e.g., IK, full-body or hand tracking). It is based on a nodal
                approach, described as a Direct Acyclic Graph, where processing is
                done successively on each note, and processed motion information
                transferred to the downstream nodes. Implementation is demonstrated on
                two main SDKs (Meta and XREAL).

    Overall, it is an interesting technical system, which is well described and seems
                to be technically sound. However, it is difficult to evaluate the
                actual contribution of the paper as the framework mostly encompasses
                existing proprietary SDKs, with a few existing animation methods
                (e.g., IK, Categorical Codebook Matching), and it is therefore
                difficult to evaluate its novelty and generalizability.

  Review

    First, I would like to point out that the paper is overall very well presented. I
                enjoyed reading the presented framework, which aims at encompassing in
                a unified system avatar animation for XR applications on heterogenous
                devices. The modular approach is well described, and seems to be
                technically sound and following good development practices (e.g.,
                description of buffers and data access in general,).

    However, despite the clear presentation of the technical work performed, I had
                difficulties identifying the novelty and the scientific contribution
                of the submission. First, it builds vastly on proprietary SDKs, but
                only demonstrates its application with Meta and XREAL SDKs. Numerous
                other approaches exist (e.g., Xsens, Manus, other trackers) and it
                difficult to evaluate the difficulty involved in implementing
                interfaces for these systems. Second, the paper presents very high-
                level information about the proposed architecture (mostly presenting
                information related to sender/receiver paradigms, buffers,
                registration), which while well-presented are quite standard
                implementation practices and novel per se. In comparison, I would have
                appreciated more detailed information about some of the difficult
                animation and retargeting nodes: do they simply encompass existing
                code? do they require to develop specific functionalities? It is also
                difficult again to evaluate how the approach would generalize to novel
                approaches that might be proposed in the future (i.e., what are the
                entry costs for developing novel nodes?)

    Finally, I think the proposed system also lacked in terms of evaluation of the
                approach. The authors presented an interesting evaluation of the
                overall latency of the system (actually concluding that disparities
                exiting between devices in terms of buffering and decentralised
                computing needs). However, I was wondering how the approach would be
                evaluated potentially in terms of usability (is it simple to change
                controlling modes in an application, how do you switch from using one
                headset to another). This would seem important as it was unclear to me
                whether this was something that should be done manually or whether it
                was transparent to the user (especially seeing the manual change
                suggested in Figure 5).

    References also seem to be overall adequate, but I think that the following work
                aiming at standardizing the use of avatars for VR applications would
                be relevant:
    Martınez, JL Pontón, E. Monclús Lahoya, and N. Pelechano Gómez. "Avatargo: plug
                and play self-avatars for vr." The European Association for Computer
                Graphics 43rd Annual Conference, EUROGRAPHICS 2022: Reims, France,
                April 25-29, 2022: short papers. 2022.

    To conclude, I think the framework presented is an interesting and well-developed
                software, but with limited scientific novelty for the conference. It
                would however fit well for a demo or poster.
