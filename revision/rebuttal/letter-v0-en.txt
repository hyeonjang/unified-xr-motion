We thank the reviewers for their careful and constructive comments.

1. Scientific contribution & expanding related work (R1, R3, R4)

We agree that missing data, validity flags, coordinate mapping, retargeting, and
incomplete skeletons are long-standing problems in motion capture, animation, and
robotics. However, our contribution is not that we solve these through one combined
system integration; it is a runtime contract and adapter boundary in which a producer
publishes into a shared schema-defined representation in an availability-gated manner,
and downstream consumers reuse it without depending on the source. The practical goal
is to reduce the burden in which, to represent their own avatar, end-users must
repeatedly write vendor- and algorithm-specific authoring and code. Even though
middleware abstracts devices and transforms, this authoring-side burden remains.

The contribution is therefore not a new estimator, retargeter, or IK algorithm, but a
systems/abstraction contribution that carries partial component availability across a
producer-consumer boundary. Its evaluation axis is not algorithmic novelty but the
design soundness and reuse properties of the boundary, in the same spirit in which
middleware and interchange abstractions are evaluated by the abstraction itself rather
than by a new algorithm. The non-obvious decision is to make availability first-class
and source-independent: downstream consumers branch on which components are present,
not on which SDK produced them, so one consumer is reused across producers it was not
written for, which is what a per-source integration cannot offer.

Following R3's suggestion, we restructured the contribution list (Sec. 1): we removed
the item that bundled reuse and setup evidence, and promoted the user study to a
standalone contribution. We also added related work on sparse tracking and VR social
co-presence and re-summarized the discussion of VR avatar motion to make the need for
the system explicit.

2. Evaluation: user study description & general XR system (R1, R2, R4)

Addressing the comment that the user study was insufficiently described, we now state
the study conditions and procedure precisely (N=19, with informed consent and
compensation) and add a table comparing the UnifiedXRMotion and vendor-SDK tasks
(Tab. 4). This within-subjects study compares
whether the reusable schema prototype, implemented through availability and the adapter
boundary, reduces setup burden, by sequentially authoring Custom-Hands and Full-body for
the two SDKs. For fairness, we specify condition-order counterbalancing, simulator
verification, and the pre-completion of hand-axis tuning. This content was already part
of the existing study guide; it does not redefine scope or derive an additional
contribution.

We agree that the evaluation is narrow. This work does not claim general developer
productivity, maintainability, runtime performance, motion quality, or broad
interoperability; these are needed to evaluate an XR system in general but are outside
our scope, and we state their absence as a limitation. We also note as limitations the
items the current record does not encode: provenance, continuous confidence, timestamps,
and schema-version negotiation.

3. Details and E2E example (R2, R3, R4)

Addressing the request for more implementation detail and reproducibility, we added a
figure showing the adapter boundary and schema structure (Fig. 2): a producer maps its
native output to schema slots, marks availability, and publishes into a shared kinematic
buffer, and a consumer then reads through the buffer view with per-component
availability-gated access. Together with the equations and tables defining
PhysicBone/PoseFlag/KinematicBuffer and region views, this allows the contract boundary
to be reproduced from the paper alone.

One reviewer suggested moving the technical detail (Sec. 3-6) to supplementary material
to shorten the body. We agree that the capabilities and user study should not be
overshadowed by technical detail. We therefore expanded the user-study description
(Tab. 4), added a figure showing the capability, and tightened repetitive wording to
reduce the narrative volume. However, moving the schema/adapter detail of Sec. 3-6 to
supplementary would conflict with R3 and R4, who requested more of that same detail; we
therefore kept it in the body but made the exposition more concise.

On calibration, we distinguish two senses. Room calibration, which aligns the coordinate
frames across tracking sources, is important in real glue code but is a separate problem
from this work: UnifiedXRMotion does not solve it and begins from the assumption that
sources are aligned to an agreed space (stated as a limitation). Avatar calibration
(aligning incoming motion to the target avatar rig), by contrast, is handled at the
adapter boundary that implements the retargeting logic, and is therefore addressed
explicitly, separately from availability.

We additionally provide a demonstration video showing the authoring pattern and the
avatar-motion result, including a runtime source swap consuming the same downstream
chain.
